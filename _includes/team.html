Studio Origins

An intuition that wouldn't rest

A studio founder in their natural habitat

"Throughout my life, different people unrelated to one another told me I reminded them of the colour red.
As a kid, I would retort "but my favourite colour is green!" — but as I grew on the path of a scientist, I started asking myself, why did multiple people think about the colour red when interacting with me? What determining factors contributed to the feeling of redness? What other visual elements would people associate with me? How consistant would it be?
This led to a few initial anecdotal experiments; I had a colleague who felt very geometric to me. In the way he thought, planned his day, approached his work, I had an intuition he would definitely look geometric if I were to paint him. So I asked around: "would you associate Dimitri with geometric shapes, or swirly ones?" and to my surprise, people eagerly exclaimed: "oh he's definitely geometric!"
How come so many people could immediately identify this connection between visual characteristics and something as intangible as a person's vibes? And then came more questions; would Dimitri still feel geometric outside of work? Would he see himself as geometric? Would his mother feel he's more of a swirly person? Perhaps he's varying degrees of both.
Sitôt's personality portraits are my attempt at formalizing these sentiments. After months of testing, I built an initial version of the software that maps who a person is to 40 different visual characteristics (think saturation, linearity, noise, contrast, curviness, etc.) and lo and behold, I scored so high on hot colours, saturation, and brightness… turns out the software thinks I'm red too."
-- Ké, studio founder

Ké is a software engineer turned full-time artist, with a tech background in machine learning, graphics, and computer vision that they have built a career with at companies like Autodesk and Microsoft. On the arts side, Ké is a classical pianist under 15 years of tutelage from Moscow Tchaikovsky Conservatory alum E. Dyachkov, as well as a published researcher as part of the Distributed Digital Music Archives and Libraries' effort to digitalize a collection of medieval square-notation sheet music through machine-led optical music recognition. However, Ké's heart has always been with visual arts, from ink on paper to paint on canvas, Ké intends on staying on the studio's artist roster even when the business paperwork starts piling up!
